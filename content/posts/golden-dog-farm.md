We didn't go to Vermont for the leaves. We went for the dogs — and we're not even a little embarrassed about that.

Golden Dog Farm sits in the Green Mountains outside Jeffersonville, about thirty minutes from Stowe. It's run by the Worple family, and on paper it's a maple syrup and honey farm — a genuinely serious one, 270-some acres, organic, the kind of place that takes its trees as seriously as anything else. But the reason it's become a whole destination is the "happy" of golden retrievers that live there. (That's the real term, by the way — a group of golden retrievers is called a "happy." We looked it up twice because we didn't believe it either.)

## The truck

You hear it before you see it. A pickup pulls up with a hand-painted "Butternut Goldens" sign on the back — the name of the breeder the farm partners with — and the truck bed is just full of golden retriever puppies, stacked up against the tailgate like they've done this a hundred times. We counted about a dozen the day we went. The second the gate drops, it's over — you're getting run at from every direction, and there is no version of this you can prepare for.

## The pond

They don't stop at the truck. The whole pack heads straight down a grassy slope to a pond on the property and goes in, all of them at once, and if you're standing anywhere nearby you're getting soaked whether you planned on it or not. Watching a dozen golden retrievers swim in formation is one of those things that sounds like it shouldn't be as funny as it is.

## Everyone gets a turn

After the swim, it's just an open field full of dogs and people — kids getting tackled with affection, someone's grandmother getting her hand licked raw, a very patient staff member toweling off one very unbothered golden while it stands there like it's being detailed at a car wash. Nobody's rushing you through it.

<div class="callout">This is a paid, reservation-only experience — no drop-ins. Book ahead directly through Golden Dog Farm's site, and go in shoulder season if you want the Green Mountains backdrop without a packed field.</div>

## Worth building a day around

If you're in the Stowe area, this isn't a twenty-minute stop — plan on it eating an afternoon, and pair it with the farm's other offerings if you can; they also run maple syrup tastings, honey tastings, and guided farm tours on the same property. We came for the puppies and left having learned more about Vermont maple syrup production than we expected to.

It's not a hidden gem in the "nobody knows about it" sense — plenty of people do. But it's the kind of stop that never makes a standard New England itinerary, and it was the best thirty minutes of the whole trip.
