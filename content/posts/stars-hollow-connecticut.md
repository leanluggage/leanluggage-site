We'd heard the rumor for years — that Stars Hollow was based on a real Connecticut town — and always assumed it was the kind of thing fans exaggerate. It isn't. Amy Sherman-Palladino wrote the Gilmore Girls pilot after staying at the Mayflower Inn in Washington, Connecticut, and a genuinely surprising amount of what she saw is still there.

## The church with the steeple

The first thing you see driving into Washington Depot is a white church with a tall steeple, sitting exactly where you'd picture Stars Hollow's town hall. It's not a replica or a set piece — it's just the actual church that's been there the whole time, doing its actual job.

## Stars Hollow Books, for real

A few doors down is The Hickory Stick Bookshop — a genuinely lovely independent bookstore that's been open more than 70 years, long before anyone was calling it "Stars Hollow Books." They lean into it now, with a dedicated Gilmore Girls section and Rory's reading list posted up, but the store itself predates the show entirely. It would be worth the stop even without the connection.

## Doose's Market has a real name too

Right around the corner: Washington Food Market, a small-town grocery store that's the visual dead ringer for Doose's Market. Someone had taped a hand-lettered "Welcome" sign to the door, which felt about right.

## The Dragonfly Inn

Up the road is the Mayflower Inn — the actual inn Sherman-Palladino stayed at while writing the pilot, and the inspiration for both the Independence Inn and the Dragonfly Inn later in the show. It's a genuinely beautiful property, gambrel roof and all, tucked behind a stone wall at the end of a gravel drive.

## The square and the gazebo

Washington's town green has its own white-steepled building looking out over a crossing, and a gazebo that could pass for the one Lorelai and Luke get married under. We stood on the gazebo steps for a very long photo session, no regrets.

## Kim's Antiques

Last stop was a little gift shop stacked with Stars Hollow tote bags and mugs — a nod to Mrs. Kim's antique shop from the show, and the easiest place in town to walk out having spent more than you planned.

<div class="callout">None of this is hidden or hard to find — it's a real town that leans into its own history happily. Budget a half day, wear comfortable shoes, and go on a weekday if you want the bookshop to yourself.</div>

We went in expecting a stretch of fan-fiction geography and left having genuinely underestimated how much of it checks out. If you grew up on this show, it's worth the drive.
